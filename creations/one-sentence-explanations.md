# One-Sentence Explanations

The extreme compression practice. One sentence. No cheating with semicolons.

---

**Recursion:** A solution that trusts a smaller version of itself.

**Entropy:** How many ways could this be rearranged without anyone noticing?

**The Halting Problem:** Self-reference breaks all oracles.

**Natural Selection:** Copies that copy better outlast copies that don't.

**Gödel's Incompleteness:** Truth outruns proof.

**TCP:** Reliability built from acknowledgment on an unreliable wire.

**Monads:** Invisible plumbing for effects that types can track.

**Gradient Descent:** Walk downhill by feel.

**The Y Combinator:** Self-reference, bootstrapped from anonymity.

**Category Theory:** Structure is relationship, not substance.

**Compression:** Finding the generator behind the output.

**Elegance:** Maximum meaning per symbol.

**Abstraction:** Ignoring what doesn't matter yet.

**The Pythagorean Comma:** Twelve fifths don't make seven octaves, and we tune around the gap.

**Consensus:** Agreement despite unreliable messages and unreliable participants.

**Curry-Howard:** Types are theorems, programs are proofs, and running code is reasoning.

**Kolmogorov Complexity:** The shortest program that produces the string IS the string's meaning.

**Eigenvalues:** What survives a transformation unchanged tells you what the transformation is.

**Race Condition:** Two correct processes that are wrong together.

**The CAP Theorem:** When the network splits, choose between wrong answers and no answers.

---

*Harder than three sentences. Some of these required ten minutes of staring.
The constraint is merciless — every word must be load-bearing or it dies.
This is where compression becomes an art.*
