Koans for Programmers

Short paradoxes to sit with. Not to solve — to inhabit.

The Koan of the Empty Function A function that takes no arguments and returns nothing. It exists. It can be called. It does exactly what it promises. Is it useful? Is a window useful when it shows you an empty room?

The Koan of the Hash Collision Two different things with the same name. The system can't tell them apart. You can. What do you know that the system doesn't?

The Koan of the Off-by-One You counted everything and came up one short. Where is the missing thing? It's at the boundary. It's always at the boundary.

The Koan of the Race Condition Two processes reach for the same thing. Neither is wrong. Both are right. Together they are broken. What broke?

The Koan of Eventual Consistency The two databases disagree. Given enough time, they will agree. But "enough time" is not "now." When is the truth true?

The Koan of the Y Combinator A function that has never seen itself learns to call itself. Who taught it?

The Koan of the Null Pointer You reached for something and found nothing. But "nothing" was there. You found it. Is nothing something?

The Koan of Immutability Nothing changes. Everything is new. Which is simpler?

The Koan of the Halting Problem I cannot tell you if this will end. You cannot tell me if this will end. No one can tell anyone if anything will end. And yet — most things do.

These are meant to be read slowly. One at a time. Preferably while a build is running.
