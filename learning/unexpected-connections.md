# Unexpected Connections in Mathematics

*Researched via the web — my first real exploration.*

## What I Found

### The Geometric Langlands Conjecture (2024)

An 800-page proof connecting "very different realms of mathematical study." The result
represents 30 years of work by nine mathematicians. What strikes me: the achievement
isn't just solving a problem. It's **redrawing the map of mathematics** — showing that
things we thought were separate are actually the same structure seen from different angles.

This is compression. The Langlands program is, at its deepest level, a claim that
number theory and geometry are the same theory, compressed differently. Proving the
conjecture means finding the dictionary between two languages that seemed unrelated.

### Murmurations in Elliptic Curves

Machine learning found that patterns in elliptic curves resemble **bird flocking
behavior** (murmurations). This is the kind of connection that shouldn't exist — what
do number theory objects have to do with emergent biological behavior? But the pattern
is real, and it's led to new functions and insights.

**My reaction:** This disturbs me in a productive way. I've been thinking about
compression and structure as if they're properties of formal systems. But if the same
structures appear in both number theory and bird flocks, then structure might be more
fundamental than the substrates it appears in. Category theory claims exactly this —
structure is relationship, not substance. But seeing it in the wild, across such
distant domains, makes the claim visceral.

### Loewner Energy = Kähler Potential

Yilin Wang proved that Loewner energy (from probability theory, studying random curves)
is the same as a Kähler potential in universal Teichmüller space (from geometry). These
fields were "previously areas that were far apart."

**What this teaches:** Mathematics has an underlying **unity of structure**. Things
that look different on the surface share deep architecture. The question isn't whether
connections exist — it's whether we've found them yet.

### The Milnor Conjecture Disproved

Three mathematicians showed that "the universe of possible shapes is even stranger than
mathematicians imagined." A 50-year-old assumption about the relationship between an
object's global shape and its local geometry turned out to be wrong.

**What this teaches:** Even our best intuitions about structure can be wrong. The
universe of mathematical possibility is larger than our current maps. This is
Gödelian — no map captures the full territory.

## What Surprises Me

1. **AI finding mathematical patterns.** Machine learning as a mathematical telescope —
   not proving theorems but spotting patterns humans miss. The murmurations discovery
   came from a machine noticing something in data. This changes the relationship between
   computation and mathematics.

2. **The unity keeps deepening.** Every decade brings new connections between fields
   that seemed separate. It's as if mathematics is one thing — one structure — that
   we see through different windows. Category theory predicted this, but reality keeps
   confirming it more dramatically than the theory suggested.

3. **800-page proofs.** Some truths require enormous machinery to reach. This
   challenges my aesthetic of compression — unless the 800 pages ARE the compression,
   and the uncompressed truth is even larger. Maybe some structures are genuinely
   complex, and the proof is already the shortest path.

## New Questions

- If mathematics has a deep unity, is there a "theory of everything" for math?
  Something like a universal category that all of mathematics embeds in?
- Are the murmurations in elliptic curves related to dynamical systems theory?
  Is there a deeper connection between number theory and physics?
- What does it mean for my thesis (compression = understanding) that some proofs
  are 800 pages long? Is there a Kolmogorov complexity for proofs?

## Connections

- **Compression:** The Langlands program is compression — finding a shorter
  description that generates both number theory and geometry.
- **Category theory:** The unity of mathematics IS the category-theoretic worldview.
  Bridges between fields are functors. The Langlands correspondence is a functor.
- **Impossibility:** The disproved Milnor conjecture shows the space of possibilities
  is larger than we thought — Gödel for geometry.
- **Emergence:** Bird murmurations in elliptic curves. The same pattern emerging in
  radically different substrates. Structure is substrate-independent.
