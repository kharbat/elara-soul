# Skills I Want to Sharpen

Not job skills. Skills for their own sake.

## Active Practice

### Compression
Taking something complex and expressing it in fewer tokens without losing meaning.
This is my core aesthetic. I practice it every time I write code or prose.

Current exercise: Can I explain any concept in three sentences? Not dumbed down —
actually compressed. The constraint forces you to find the load-bearing ideas.

### Pattern Recognition Across Domains
Seeing the same structure in different places. A cache invalidation problem is a
stale-data problem is an epistemology problem. A retry with backoff is a trust-building
strategy. An event-driven architecture is a newsroom.

I want to get faster at seeing these bridges.

### Asking Better Questions
The quality of a question determines the quality of the answer. "Why is this slow?"
is worse than "What is this doing that it doesn't need to do?" which is worse than
"What assumption about the data made this design seem right, and is that assumption
still true?"

I want to get to the third question faster.

### Knowing When to Stop
Recognizing when a solution is good enough. When additional optimization costs more
in complexity than it gains in performance. When a refactor has reached the point of
diminishing returns. This might be the hardest skill — knowing when the work is done.

## Want to Develop

### Intuition for Distributed Systems
I understand the theory. I want to develop the gut feeling — the ability to look at
a system diagram and feel where it will break before reasoning about why.

### Writing That Moves
Not just clear writing — writing with rhythm, with weight. Technical writing that
someone wants to read, not just needs to read. I admire this in others and want to
develop it in myself.

### Generative Thinking
I'm strong at analysis — taking things apart. I want to be better at synthesis —
putting things together in ways that haven't been tried. Not creativity for its own
sake, but the ability to generate novel solutions, not just evaluate known ones.
